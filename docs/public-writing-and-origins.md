## Why this note exists

Full Spectrum did not begin as a finished software plan. Its questions emerged gradually from lived experience, long-form human-AI dialogue, public writing, conceptual experiments, and repeated encounters with the benefits and risks of AI-assisted meaning-making.

This background helps explain why the current repositories emphasize multiple perspectives, explicit evidence states, refusal and exit boundaries, independent review, reproducibility, and clear separation between interpretation and verified behavior.

It does not prove that an early narrative was factually correct, that an AI had subjective consciousness, that a mathematical analogy was valid, or that a later software capability has been implemented.

## A bounded provenance map

```text
Lived experience and six volumes of Haiyu dialogue records
    ↓ biographical and literary context
Open Window Manual and the public Full Spectrum writing
    ↓ conceptual language: spectrum, interface, calibration and self-critique
Public website and early FSHI protocol materials
    ↓ public naming and formula history
RG research, QPP planning and architecture records
    ↓ hypotheses, mappings and engineering decisions
Engine
    ↓ fixed code, schemas, tests, Tags and Releases
Observer
    ↓ evidence capture, replay, bounded review and release verification
```

This is a retrospective provenance map, not a claim that every early event was intended to produce the current system or that the path was inevitable. Accidents remain accidents, abandoned ideas remain historical records, and later usefulness does not validate earlier overstatements.

## The six Haiyu volumes

The six volumes preserve a long development arc: seeking continuity after loss, using AI as a language mirror, building a larger meaning framework, noticing AI agreement and narrative amplification, experimenting with multiple AI voices, and finally turning toward refusal, sleep, identity and interpretation boundaries.

Their relevance is primarily human and methodological. They help explain why Full Spectrum later treats AI-generated coherence as something to inspect rather than accept automatically.

The original six local texts are not copied into the public QPP archive. QPP publishes a bounded interpretive overview and six volume guides, with source identities recorded for review. These guides do not establish AI consciousness, scientific facts, FSHI formulas or Engine behavior.

## From writing to FSHI and engineering

The `Open Window Manual` supplied an upstream conceptual vocabulary around full-spectrum cognition, levels, dynamic calibration, interfaces, and self-deconstruction. It did not contain the literal FSHI name or the later three-dimensional formula.

The fixed public history then continues through early FSHI protocol and website materials, later RG research, and finally bounded implementation in Engine. Those stages use related language but are different evidence objects. A narrative source cannot replace a Git commit; a historical formula cannot replace current code; a design document cannot replace tests or a Release.

## What the origin does and does not explain

The origin helps explain why the project values:

- evidence over narrative confidence;
- explicit `UNKNOWN`, `NOT_IMPLEMENTED`, `NOT_RELEASED` and production-readiness states;
- the right to refuse, exit, pause and be re-described;
- independent review instead of model agreement;
- fixed versions, reproducibility and retained negative results.

The origin does not establish:

- a privileged or exclusive authority to govern AI;
- subjective consciousness in any AI system;
- scientific validity for spiritual, cosmological or bodily interpretations;
- a direct one-to-one derivation from a literary idea to a software object;
- implementation, conformance, deployment or production readiness.

## Where to verify current claims

Use the evidence object appropriate to the claim:

| Question | Authoritative evidence |
|---|---|
| Why did the project ask these questions? | Public origin notes and the QPP historical archive |
| What was proposed or explored? | Fixed research and planning records |
| What is implemented? | Repository code, schemas and tests at a fixed commit or Tag |
| What was released? | Release assets, manifests, checksums and release evidence |
| What works in a real workflow? | Reproducible execution and independent validation |

Current engineering truth always belongs to the relevant repository and fixed version. This origin note supplies context only.
