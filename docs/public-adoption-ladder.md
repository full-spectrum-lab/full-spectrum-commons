# Public Adoption Ladder

This document explains how Full Spectrum can be adopted without forcing subjects, companies, or communities to join a full protocol network on day one.

## Short version

Full Spectrum should be adopted in layers:

1. **Internal engine use**
2. **Cell protocol declaration**
3. **Cross-node protocol connection**
4. **Guardian and contribution network**

Each step adds more coordination power, but also more responsibility.

---

## Stage 1: Internal engine use

What happens here:

- one company, team, or developer runs a local engine internally;
- synthetic or desensitized cases are inspected;
- RiskAlert, AuditTrace, Runestone, and report outputs are generated;
- no external protocol network is required.

Typical benefit:

- internal visibility;
- local QA and compliance support;
- low adoption friction;
- proof that governance outputs are useful before any broader commitment.

Relevant repositories:

- [full-spectrum-engine](https://github.com/full-spectrum-lab/full-spectrum-engine)
- [full-spectrum-enterprise-governance](https://github.com/full-spectrum-lab/full-spectrum-enterprise-governance)

---

## Stage 2: Cell protocol declaration

What happens here:

- a subject declares who or what it is;
- capability and boundary are made explicit;
- permission and responsibility become reviewable;
- internal governance becomes more structured even if the system still operates inside one organization.

This is where a company, team, or agent starts saying:

> this is my declared role, this is what I can do, this is what I cannot do, and this is how my actions should be reviewed.

Relevant repository:

- [full-spectrum-protocol](https://github.com/full-spectrum-lab/full-spectrum-protocol)

---

## Stage 3: Cross-node protocol connection

What happens here:

- multiple subjects or organizations need to exchange audit-compatible records;
- cross-enterprise audit envelopes matter;
- compatibility profiles, review interfaces, and responsibility paths become necessary.

This stage is not required for early internal adoption, but it becomes important when:

- one company wants to prove governance posture to another;
- internal AI decisions produce external consequences;
- multiple systems need interoperable audit records.

Relevant repository:

- [full-spectrum-protocol](https://github.com/full-spectrum-lab/full-spectrum-protocol)

---

## Stage 4: Guardian and contribution network

What happens here:

- stronger review structures appear;
- guardian review or distributed review can be introduced;
- contribution records and community-level norms become more visible;
- protocol-network questions move from local governance into ecosystem governance.

This is later-stage work and should not block earlier adoption.

---

## Why this ladder matters

Without a layered path, Full Spectrum would be too heavy for first-time users.

The practical rule is:

> Start by helping one node solve one real internal governance problem.

Then, only after value is proven:

- add stronger declarations,
- add stronger compatibility,
- add stronger network coordination.

---

## What not to force too early

Do not require first-time adopters to accept all of the following immediately:

- a global protocol network;
- guardian review structures;
- full certification;
- community contribution logic;
- protocol-wide frequency economy.

Those belong later, after local usefulness is demonstrated.
